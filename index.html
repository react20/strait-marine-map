Tide & moon calendar

Tide heights in metres above chart datum (lowest normal tide), from the Canadian Hydrographic Service. ▲ high (blue) and ▼ low tide, with time (24-hour) and height in metres. Moon phases are calculated and accurate to within about half a day. Each day's small chart is the tide from midnight to midnight (same scale all month). Its colour shows how fast the tide is changing, a guide to current strength: red strong (over 0.6 m/h), orange moderate, green weak, near slack water. Each day is split into four 6-hour blocks (00–06, 06–12, 12–18, 18–24): each block shows the wind (arrow and knots: green under 10, orange 10–20, red over 20), then the temperature and weather icon, and under the tide curve the estimated wave height in metres (green under 0.5, orange 0.5–1, red over 1), for the next 14 days. Days 1–7 use Environment Canada's town forecast; days 8–10 the global model (GDPS); days 11–14 the ensemble average (GEPS), which gives wind speed without direction, so those days have no arrow or wave estimate. Past blocks are shown greyed out with what was actually measured at the nearest weather station (Point Atkinson or Entrance Island): temperature, wind, rain icon when rain fell, and waves estimated from that wind. Wind and waves are for open water just off the chosen tide station. Waves are estimated from the forecast wind and how far it blows over open water; no ocean swell reaches the inner Strait. Hover a block for details. Actual currents in passes can differ, so check CHS current tables for Gabriola Passage and other narrows. Hover a block to see where its numbers came from.
